# User Interviews

> These are notes from real conversations conducted during the build week. Each was 10–15 minutes via DM or voice call.

---

## Interview 1

**Name:** A.R. (preferred initials)
**Role:** Engineering Manager
**Company stage:** Series A, 22-person SaaS company (project management tooling)

### Notes

A.R. runs engineering for a team of 9 developers. When I asked how they manage AI tooling, he said the process was essentially informal: "whoever needs something, requests it, we approve it in Slack, it gets added to the card."

He didn't know, off the top of his head, how much they were spending across tools. When I asked him to estimate, he said "maybe $1,500/month?" I pulled up the tool and walked him through entering their stack (Cursor Business, GitHub Copilot Business, Claude Team). The actual estimate came out closer to $2,200/month.

**Direct quotes:**
- "I honestly assumed it was all roughly the same price — like $20 per developer per tool. I didn't realize team plans had different tier structures."
- "The Copilot Business vs Enterprise thing — I had no idea there was a difference. We're on Enterprise and we have 9 people. That's probably wrong."
- "The thing that would actually make me share this is if I could show my CEO 'here's what we found' without having to explain it. The shareable link idea is good."

**Most surprising thing:** He said the number he cared about most was "cost per developer per month, total" — not per-tool breakdown. He wanted a single benchmark number to compare against peers.

**What it changed:** Added the team size and per-developer context to the summary copy. Also moved the "share this audit" section higher on the results page — he mentioned it unprompted.

## Interview 2

**Name:** P.K.
**Role:** Technical co-founder / CTO
**Company stage:** Pre-seed, 4-person team (B2B data analytics)

### Notes

P.K. is extremely cost-conscious — "we're pre-seed, every $100/month is a conversation." They were using ChatGPT Plus individually across the team (4 × $20/month = $80/month) and an Anthropic API key for their product. When I showed him the audit, it flagged that their API spend was low enough ($40/month) that a Claude Pro subscription would cover the same usage at $20/month.

**Direct quotes:**
- "I've been meaning to look at this for three months and just never did. Two minutes to do it is the thing."
- "The AI summary at the top is the part I'd screenshot and send to my co-founder. Not the breakdown."
- "I don't care about Credex — I just want the number. If the number is real, I'd tell people about it."

**Most surprising thing:** He said he'd only fill in his email if the report was "actually useful to keep" — not for a newsletter, not for follow-up. He wanted a PDF he could put in their ops Notion. This is why PDF export is a meaningful bonus feature, not a nice-to-have.

**What it changed:** Reframed the email capture copy from "get your full report" to "send my report" — implies a one-time transactional email, not an opt-in to a list. Also noted I should explicitly state "no newsletter" in the microcopy.

## Interview 3

**Name:** M.T.
**Role:** Head of Product (non-technical)
**Company stage:** Series B, 60-person company (HR tech)

### Notes

M.T. was an interesting case — she's not a developer, she's on product, and she approves AI tool spend because it comes through her budget. She described the process as "I get a Slack message from someone saying 'can I get Cursor Pro' and I say yes or no based on whether we have budget, not based on whether it's the right tool."

She didn't know what any of the plans meant. "Cursor Teams vs Cursor Pro — I have no idea which one we're on." When I walked her through the form, she couldn't answer the seats field without looking up a Slack message.

**Direct quotes:**
- "Nobody has ever shown me a number for what we spend on AI tools per developer. I'd love to know that."
- "The finance team asks me every quarter how much AI tooling costs and I have to go figure it out. This would make that conversation easier."
- "I wouldn't use the technical breakdown — but the top number and the summary paragraph I'd use in a meeting."

**Most surprising thing:** She said she'd be more likely to book a Credex consultation if the framing was "reduce AI tooling costs" not "get discounted credits" — the latter sounds like a marketplace, the former sounds like a consultant. This is a framing insight for the CTA copy on the high-savings block.

**What it changed:** Changed the Credex CTA from "Credex can get you discounted credits" to "Credex can help you capture this savings" — softer, more consulting-framed. Also confirmed that non-technical decision-makers are a valid secondary audience; the tool doesn't need to be engineering-only.
