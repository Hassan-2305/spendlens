# Go-to-Market Plan

## Exact Target User

**Engineering Manager or CTO at a 5–40 person B2B SaaS startup, Series A or pre-Series A, US/UK.**

More specifically: someone who approved buying Cursor last year, then approved GitHub Copilot for the same team six months later, and never thought about whether both were necessary for the same developer workflow. They're not the person who signs up for tools — that's the developer — but they're the person who sees the monthly consolidated bill and wonders if this is normal.

Job title: "Engineering Manager," "VP Engineering," "Head of Engineering," or a technical co-founder with fewer than 40 engineers reporting to them.

Company stage signal: They use Stripe, they use Vercel or AWS, they use Linear or Jira. Their SaaS product has paying customers. They're past ramen mode but not past "every dollar matters."

---

## What They're Doing Before They Find SpendLens

They're not Googling "AI tool spend audit" — they don't know that's a category. They're Googling:
- "is cursor pro worth it"
- "github copilot vs cursor 2025"
- "chatgpt team vs enterprise cost"
- "how much does AI tooling cost per developer"

Or they're scrolling through their Ramp or Brex dashboard and seeing line items they approved but can't quite recall why.

Or a developer on their team complains that their Cursor credits ran out mid-month, and they Google "cursor pro plan limits."

These are the entry points. Not "audit my AI spend" — the realization that comes *before* the category exists.

## First 100 Users in 30 Days — Zero Budget

**Week 1: Seeding via HN**

Post "Show HN: I built a free AI spend auditor — found $340/mo in overspend for my own team." Include actual numbers from a real audit (run SpendLens on a realistic 8-person stack: Cursor Teams + Copilot Business + Claude Team + ChatGPT Plus). Post on a Tuesday morning (highest HN engagement). A Show HN that surfaces a real pain point and includes actual numbers reliably gets 50–200 upvotes. That's 2,000–10,000 visitors in 48 hours.

**Week 2: Twitter/X thread**

Post a breakdown of the audit: "We audited a 10-person engineering team's AI stack. Here's what we found." Thread format, with screenshots of the results page. Tag relevant accounts without being spammy. This is the format that engineers share — specific numbers, not generic advice.

**Week 3: Reddit**

Post in r/ExperiencedDevs: "Built a quick tool to audit AI tool spend — curious what others are seeing." Not promotional framing. Frame it as a personal project solving a problem you had. The community will engage if it's genuinely useful.

**Week 4: Direct outreach**

Use LinkedIn to identify 20 Engineering Managers at 15-40 person SaaS companies. DM with: "Hey — I built a 2-minute AI spend auditor. Ran it on a similar-sized team and found $400/mo in overspend. Happy to share the link if you want to try it on your stack." Conversion at 5/20 gives you qualified leads who've seen the tool work.

## The Unfair Distribution Channel

Credex already has relationships with AI tool vendors. The one channel no random indie hacker has: **Credex can reach overbuying customers through the vendors themselves.**

Specifically: companies that purchased ChatGPT Enterprise or Cursor Enterprise directly from the vendor, then reduced headcount or pivoted, now have unused credits or overprovisioned plans. Credex has contacts at these vendors. A quiet co-marketing arrangement — "SpendLens helps your customers right-size, which builds trust, and Credex helps them redeploy savings into smarter credit purchases" — is a channel no cold-traffic tool can replicate.
